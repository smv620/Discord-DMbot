## What is here

`spells.json`, `conditions.json`, `equipment.json`, `features.json`, `feats.json`, `items.json`, `origins.json`, `rules.json`, `toolbox.json` and `monsters.json` hold every spell, every condition, every class and subclass feature (the Classes chapter), every race (the Races chapter), the one background (Acolyte) and the one feat (Grappler), the weapons, armor, weapon properties, gear, tools, mounts and vehicles (the Equipment chapter), every magic item (the Magic Items chapter) and every creature of the SRD 5.1 (the 2014 rules: the Monsters pages, the Miscellaneous Creatures appendix and the Nonplayer Characters appendix), and nothing from any other book. DMbot uses them only as the legacy fallback, tagged `[Legacy 2014]`, when the newer rules have nothing under the name. Each file says where it came from: the SRD 5.1 PDF that Wizards of the Coast publishes at the address in the file, with that PDF's SHA-256, so anyone can check it.

## Changes made (CC BY 4.0 asks us to say so)

The words are the SRD's. DMbot's own tool (`python -m dmbot.devtools.srd`) took them out of the PDF and:

- removed the stray tabs, no-break spaces and soft hyphens in the PDF's text, and closed up the spaces around hyphens ("15 - foot- radius" is "15-foot-radius");
- split the Classes chapter into its features (each heading that the class table names, with its description, filed under its class and the level the table gives; the subclass's features under the subclass, with the level their first sentence names). A heading that is not a feature ("Cantrips", "Ritual Casting", the pacts of the Warlock) is kept as a line of the feature above it. Left out: the class tables, the proficiencies and the starting equipment, the lists of options (the fighting styles, the metamagic options, the eldritch invocations), the introductions to the subclasses and each subclass's own introduction and spell list, and the flavour sections after a class;
- split the Magic Items chapter into its items (one for each heading the SRD gives, 239 in all; a name that covers several bonuses, such as “Armor, +1, +2, or +3”, is one entry, as printed), read the italic line under each name into its category, the category’s bracket, its rarity and whether it needs attunement and by whom, and wrote the category and the rarity with the capitals the 5.2.1 files use (“Wondrous item, very rare” is “Wondrous Item”, “Very Rare”; the Spell Scroll’s “varies” is “Rarity Varies”). An item keeps the rarity this edition prints, even where the 5.2.1 prints another (the Potion of Invisibility is very rare here). Left out of the chapter: its introduction (the sections Magic Items, Attunement, Wearing and Wielding Items, Multiple Items of the Same Kind, Paired Items, Activating an Item, Command Word, Consumables, Spells and Charges: rules about items, not items), its Sentient Magic Items section (Creating Sentient Magic Items, Abilities, Communication, Senses, Alignment, Special Purpose and Conflict), its Artifacts section (the Orb of Dragonkind, the one artifact), and the tables inside an item (the 1d100 effects of the Bag of Beans, the Apparatus of the Crab Levers, ...) and the stat blocks printed in the Deck of Many Things (the Avatar of Death), because their cells and lines do not read as one item’s sentences. The 30 items with something left out are: Apparatus of the Crab, Armor of Resistance, Bag of Beans, Bag of Tricks, Belt of Giant Strength, Candle of Invocation, Carpet of Flying, Cube of Force, Deck of Illusions, Deck of Many Things, Dragon Scale Mail, Efreeti Bottle, Elemental Gem, Feather Token, Figurine of Wondrous Power, Horn of Valhalla, Iron Flask, Manual of Golems, Necklace of Prayer Beads, Potion of Giant Strength, Potion of Healing, Potion of Resistance, Ring of Resistance, Ring of Shooting Stars, Robe of Useful Items, Spell Scroll, Sphere of Annihilation, Staff of Power, Staff of the Magi, Wand of Wonder (a test pins their number);
- split the Races chapter into its 9 races (Dwarf, Elf, Halfling, Human, Dragonborn, Gnome, Half-Elf, Half-Orc, Tiefling), each as one entry of the kind “species” in the 5.2.1’s shape, with its size and its walking speed read from its Size and Speed traits and its traits as one paragraph each; a subrace (the Hill Dwarf, the High Elf, the Lightfoot Halfling, the Rock Gnome) stays inside its race’s entry under its own heading line, not as an entry of its own, and the creature type, which the SRD 5.1 does not give a race, is empty. Left out: the chapter’s Racial Traits introduction (Ability Score Increase, Age, Alignment, Size, Speed and Languages as general rules, and Subraces) and the Draconic Ancestry table of the Dragonborn, because its cells do not read as sentences (the Dragonborn’s text ends with the line “(The Draconic Ancestry table is not included here.)”, as the 5.2.1 species do, so no one takes the table for missing from the rules). A subrace’s heading line begins “Subrace: ”, and the Acolyte’s three labelled lines are put first, as the 5.2.1 backgrounds have them. The one background, Acolyte, is an entry of the kind “background” with its skill proficiencies, languages and equipment read from its three lines (the SRD 5.1 gives it no ability scores, feat or tool, so those are empty), its words and its feature (Shelter of the Faithful); left out of it are its Suggested Characteristics (the tables of personality traits, ideals, bonds and flaws) and the chapter’s general text on backgrounds. The one feat, Grappler, has its prerequisite read from its italic line and no category, because the SRD 5.1 gives feats none; the Feats chapter’s introduction is left out;
- split the Equipment chapter into its 37 weapons (category, damage and type, properties, weight and cost; the SRD 5.1 has no weapon mastery, so that field is empty), its 12 armors and the Shield (13 entries; Armor Class as printed, Strength, Stealth, weight, cost and, from the Donning and Doffing table, the time to don and to doff), its 11 weapon properties (as entries of the kind “rule” tagged Weapon Property; the Reach property is named “Reach (Weapon Property)” as in the 5.2.1 files), its 86 adventuring gear entries (each with its price and weight from the Adventuring Gear table and its words as printed; a table row with no paragraph, such as the Abacus, says its row in words; the small tables of the Ammunition, Arcane focus, Druidic focus and Holy symbol are one entry each with their rows as lines; “Rope” is one entry that lists the hempen and the silk), its 25 tools (price and weight from the Tools table and the paragraph about the tool; the artisan’s tools share the paragraph about them; the Gaming Set and the Musical Instrument list their kinds as variants, and the SRD 5.1 gives tools no ability, Utilize or Craft lines, so those are empty) and its 19 mounts and vehicles (8 mounts with speed and carrying capacity, the 5 vehicles pulled by an animal with weight and price, the 6 waterborne vehicles with speed and price). Names are written with capitals as the 5.2.1 files write theirs (“Crossbow, Light”), prices in capitals (“15 GP”), and a weapon’s properties as in the 5.2.1 (“Thrown (Range 20/60)”); a rule or rate is read from the rows as printed. The Lance’s and the Net’s rules from the Special Weapons section are kept with those two weapons. Left out of the chapter: the introduction and the coins (with the Standard Exchange Rates table), Selling Treasure, the text on armor (Armor Proficiency, Armor Class, Heavy Armor, Stealth and Getting Into and Out of Armor) and on weapon proficiency, the Improvised Weapons and Silvered Weapons sections, the Container Capacity table, the Equipment Packs, the Vehicles row of the Tools table and the Vehicle Proficiency text, the rows and text for Barding, Bit and bridle, the saddles, Saddlebags, Feed and Stabling, the Rowed Vessels note, and the Trade Goods, Expenses (Lifestyle Expenses, Food, Drink, and Lodging, Services, Spellcasting Services), because they are rules about buying and hiring rather than things or their tables do not read as sentences;
- split the core rules chapters (`rules.json`, section "Rules": Using Ability Scores, Adventuring, Combat, and Spellcasting up to its spell lists, pages 76 to 104) into 164 rules, one for each title the PDF sets in its semi-bold sans font, with the words under it up to the next title (the PDF has no heading levels, so a rule such as Combat's Making an Attack holds only its own introduction and each rule inside it is its own entry). A title that comes more than once is told apart by its place and named for it: the five skill lists under Skills (Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma, each with its bullets) are lines of the Skills rule, and the second Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma, the two Attack Rolls and Damage, the two Initiative, Hit Points, Difficult Terrain, Reactions, Attack Rolls, Range and Saving Throws, and the three Spellcasting Ability keep the first's name for the one that is the same rule in the 5.2.1's glossary and add the place in brackets to the other ("Hit Points (Constitution)", "Difficult Terrain (Combat)", "Range (Spells)"). Much of this part of the PDF is set in the sans fonts (Calibri) as well as the serif ones, so the tool reads both as prose; it put back a minus sign the PDF draws a line above its number ("from −5"), the modifier letter apostrophe (read as the ordinary one) and a combining stroke where the book has an em dash. Left out of the chapters: their two openings (the introductions under Using Ability Scores and Spellcasting, 19 and 7 lines) and the four tables (Ability Scores and Modifiers, Typical Difficulty Classes, Travel Pace and Size Categories), because their cells do not read as sentences; the tool stops if what it leaves out changes. Seventeen titles are spelled another way in the 5.2.1's glossary: seven renames (Suffocating, Use an Object, Cast a Spell, Proficiency Bonus, Creature Size, Knocking a Creature Out, Stabilizing a Creature) and ten plurals (Attack Rolls, Damage Rolls, Critical Hits, Saving Throws, Death Saving Throws, Cantrips, Rituals, Reactions, Bonus Actions, Areas of Effect): `aliases.py` finds the 2024 entry by the 5.1's title, and the 5.1 rule by the 5.2.1's in a 2014 campaign, so a 2024 campaign never gets the 2014 rule over it;
- split the game master chapters Traps, Diseases, Madness, Objects and Poisons (`toolbox.json`, section "Gameplay Toolbox" as the 5.2.1's, pages 195 to 205) into 17 rules (Traps, Traps in Play, Triggering a Trap, Detecting and Disabling a Trap, Trap Effects, Complex Traps, Diseases, Madness, Going Mad, Madness Effects, Curing Madness, Objects, Statistics for Objects, Poisons, and the three madness tables, Short-Term, Long-Term and Indefinite Madness, as a rule each), 11 traps (the sample traps; the Pits section is four traps, Simple, Hidden, Locking and Spiked Pit, each under its own lead-in), 3 diseases (Cackle Fever, Sewer Plague, Sight Rot) and 14 poisons (each with its type and price from the Poisons table and its words from the lead-in under Sample Poisons; the tool stops if the two disagree). The tables in the sans fonts became one line for each row: the Trap Save DCs and Attack Bonuses and the Damage Severity by Level (in Trap Effects), the Object Armor Class and Object Hit Points (in Statistics for Objects), and the three madness tables (rows of "01–20: ..." under the table's own heading). Left out: the titles Sample Traps and Sample Diseases, which only introduce what is below them, and the one sentence "Four basic pit traps are presented here."; the Poisons price table's caption, which is read into the poisons; the build stops if what it leaves out changes. A trap the 5.2.1 renamed is found by the 5.1's name (Poison Darts is Poisoned Darts, Poison Needle is Poisoned Needle, Rolling Sphere is Rolling Stone, Fire-Breathing Statue is Fire-Casting Statue), and the other way round in a 2014 campaign;
- joined the lines of each paragraph, and put words back together where the PDF's text had cut them with a stray space ("hig her" is "higher", "atta cked" is "attacked"). A space is taken out when the joined word is one the SRD uses (the words of the 5.2.1 data and the words this PDF uses often), or when neither piece is a word and the PDF has the joined word whole elsewhere (“exc ess”, with “excess” on another page); two real words the SRD merely does not use (“gum arabic”, “rotten egg”) are never joined on a guess. Two words that are both words are joined when they make a more common word, the 5.2.1 data never has them side by side, and this PDF has the joined word at least twenty times as often as the pair (“dark vision”, “drag on”, “an other”; “up on” and “has ten” are the book's own and stay). Where the PDF put a letter on the wrong side of a space, the letter is moved across ("the n ature" is "the nature", "a t arget" is "a target", "forth e duration" is "for the duration"). A hyphen at the end of a line is dropped when the word without it is one the SRD uses and the hyphenated word is not;
- closed the space the PDF leaves before a full stop, comma, semicolon or colon;
- split the text into entries, and pulled each spell's level, school, casting time, range, components and duration out of its heading lines (this edition's spell heading has no list of classes, so these entries have none);
- recorded the page each entry is on, so DMbot can name it ("SRD 5.1, Spell Descriptions, p. 114");
- read "Component:" in Contagion's heading as "Components:";
- read each creature's stat block into fields (size, type and alignment, armor class and what follows it, hit points and their dice, speed, the six ability scores, saving throws, skills, damage vulnerabilities, resistances and immunities, condition immunities, senses, languages, challenge rating and XP), and kept the traits and actions as one paragraph each under their headings (Actions, Reactions, Legendary Actions), with a spell list line by line. The group names in front of a creature ("Fungi", "Golems") and the book's own paragraphs between stat blocks are not part of any entry;
- closed a space inside a die ("3 d10" is "3d10") and a number the print had split ("17d10 + 8 5" is "17d10 + 85", "3, 900 XP" is "3,900 XP", "+1 0 to hit" is "+10 to hit").

Tables inside a spell or condition are kept as plain lines of text, one line for each row (a row that the print wraps over two lines is joined, so the words of a wrapped cell come at the end of the row).

## Known defects of the PDF

The PDF's text layer has lost a few words and letters altogether, and two defects are left in the files, neither corrected by hand (the files hold what the PDF holds):

- Animal Friendship's “At Higher Levels” line reads “one additional beast t level above 1st”, a word lost for good;

Where a spell's or creature's wording matters, check it against the SRD 5.1 itself. A few plain words (“attacked”, “defends”, “dread”, “dimly”, “lit”, “bed”, “linen”, “strip”, “ideas”) are listed in the tool as known words, because neither the 5.2.1 data nor this PDF spells them out often enough for the cut-word reading to know them.

The `document` block of each file records the SHA-256 of the 5.2.1 data files whose words the tool used (`word_list_sha256`), so the same PDF gives the same files only against the same 5.2.1 data (and the same tool: its short list of known words and its thresholds are in `parse51.py`).

## Making the files again

Download the PDF into a folder of its own (it is not kept in this repository), then, from `core/`:

    pip install -e ".[srd]"
    python -m dmbot.devtools.srd PATH/TO/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf

The same PDF always gives the same files, so running the tool on the PDF and seeing no change (`git diff`) is how to check them. The tool recognizes which of the two SRDs it was given, and stops with a message rather than guessing when the layout is not what it expects.
