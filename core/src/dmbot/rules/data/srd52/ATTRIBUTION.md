## What is here

`spells.json`, `conditions.json`, `rules.json`, `toolbox.json`, `features.json`, `feats.json`, `origins.json`, `equipment.json`, `items.json` and `monsters.json` hold every spell, every condition, every other entry of the Rules Glossary (its Rules Definitions: Cover, the Dash action, Long Rest, ...), every class and subclass feature of the Classes chapter (Rage, Evasion, ...) and every feat of the Feats chapter, every species and background of the Character Origins chapter, every weapon, every armor and the Shield, and every weapon property and mastery property of the Equipment chapter, every magic item of the Magic Items A–Z chapter and every creature (the Monsters A–Z and Animals sections), and every poison, every sample trap, the one named curse and the rules of the Gameplay Toolbox's travel, curses and contagions, environmental effects, fear and mental stress, poison and traps sections of the SRD 5.2.1, and nothing from any other book. Each file says where it came from: the SRD 5.2.1 PDF that Wizards of the Coast publishes at the address in the file, with that PDF's SHA-256, so anyone can check it.

## Changes made (CC BY 4.0 asks us to say so)

The words are the SRD's. DMbot's own tool (`python -m dmbot.devtools.srd`) took them out of the PDF and:

- joined the lines of each paragraph and put back words the print had cut at the end of a line;
- split the text into entries, and pulled each spell's level, school, classes, casting time, range, components and duration out of its heading lines;
- recorded the page each entry is on, so DMbot can name it ("SRD 5.2.1, Spell Descriptions, p. 131");
- read three oddities as they were meant: the title of Acid Splash and the ability names in the stat blocks inside spells are set in small capitals, which come out of the PDF in mixed case ("Acid SplASh", "dex", "WiS"), so the title is written in ordinary capitals and the ability names as Str, Dex, Con, Int, Wis and Cha; and Barkskin's line says "Component:" for "Components:";
- read each creature's stat block into fields: size, type and alignment from the italic line, armor class and initiative, hit points and their dice, speed, the six ability scores (the table's modifiers and saves are read too; only the saving throws that differ from the modifier are kept, as “Dex +5, Wis +3”, the way the 5.1 prints them), skills, gear, resistances, vulnerabilities, immunities (damage and conditions together, as printed), senses, languages, challenge rating, XP and proficiency bonus; and kept the traits and actions as one paragraph each under their headings (Traits, Actions, Bonus Actions, Reactions, Legendary Actions), with a spell list line by line;
- left out of each Rules Glossary entry its tables (their cells do not read as sentences) and its “See also” line, which is kept apart as a list of the entries it points to;
- read the PDF's text with pypdfium2 (#1892), which keeps all of it (pypdf and pdfminer.six leave parts out: the ability table's Dex, Con, Wis and Cha labels, some numbers, an equals sign), and put back the space the PDF draws as a gap (before a number, between pieces, and the indent that starts a paragraph);
- split the Classes chapter into its features (each `Level N: Name` with its description, filed under its class and, for a subclass's, its subclass) and left out of it the class tables, the spell lists, the option lists (Metamagic, Eldritch Invocations, Beast Shapes) and each subclass's introduction, because their cells and lists do not read as one feature's sentences;
- split the Magic Items A–Z chapter into its items (one for each heading the SRD gives, 258 in all; a name that covers several bonuses, such as “Armor, +1, +2, or +3” or a family such as “Potions of Healing”, is one entry, as printed), read the italic line under each name into its category, the category’s bracket, its rarity as printed (“Uncommon (+1), Rare (+2), or Very Rare (+3)”) and whether it needs attunement and by whom, and left out of the chapter its introduction (rules about items, not items), the tables inside an item (the 1d100 effects of the Bag of Beans, the Apparatus of the Crab Levers, ...) and the stat blocks printed inside an item (the Giant Fly of the Figurine of Wondrous Power, the Avatar of Death of the Mysterious Deck), because their cells and lines do not read as one item’s sentences. The 41 items with something left out are: Ammunition of Slaying, Amulet of the Planes, Apparatus of the Crab, Armor of Resistance, Bag of Beans, Bag of Tricks, Belt of Giant Strength, Candle of Invocation, Carpet of Flying, Cube of Force, Deck of Illusions, Dragon Orb, Dragon Scale Mail, Efreeti Bottle, Elemental Gem, Feather Token, Figurine of Wondrous Power, Hat of Many Spells, Horn of Valhalla, Manual of Golems, Mysterious Deck, Necklace of Prayer Beads, Potion of Giant Strength, Potions of Healing, Potion of Resistance, Ring of Elemental Command, Ring of Resistance, Ring of Shooting Stars, Robe of Useful Items, Spell Scroll, Sphere of Annihilation, Staff of Fire, Staff of Frost, Staff of Healing, Staff of Power, Staff of Swarming Insects, Staff of the Magi, Staff of the Woodlands, Wand of Binding, Wand of Fear and Wand of Wonder (a test pins the list);
- split the Feats chapter into its 17 feats (each with its category and, if it has one, its prerequisite, read from the italic line under its name) and left out of it the introduction and the four category headings; split the Character Origins chapter into its 4 backgrounds (their five lines, as printed: ability scores, feat, skill proficiencies, tool proficiency and equipment, with the feat also named on its own) and its 9 species (size, speed and creature type read from their lines, then their traits, one paragraph each), and left out of it the chapter’s introductions (“Character Backgrounds”, “Parts of a Background”, “Character Species”, “Parts of a Species”, which are rules about building a character, not entries) and three tables inside the species, because their cells do not read as sentences: the Draconic Ancestors (Dragonborn), the Elven Lineages (Elf) and the Fiendish Legacies (Tiefling), ending each of those three species with the line “(The … table is not included here.)” so no one takes a lineage’s speed or spell for missing from the rules;
- split the Equipment chapter's Weapons table into its 38 weapons (category, damage and type, properties as printed with their range and versatile numbers, mastery, weight and cost) and its Armor table into its 12 armors and the Shield (category, Armor Class as printed, Strength, Stealth, weight, cost and the time to don or doff), read each table row (whose last cells the PDF prints apart from the rest of the row) back into one row, and wrote each row out as words; took the 10 weapon properties and the 8 mastery properties as entries of their own, each word for word (the weapon property Reach is named “Reach (Weapon Property)” because the Rules Glossary already has a Reach, a creature’s, which is kept as it is); took the 25 tools (each with its price, ability, weight, and its Utilize, Craft and Variants lines as printed), the 81 adventuring gear entries (each with its price from its title, its weight from the Adventuring Gear table and its words as printed; the small tables inside an entry, such as the Arcane Focuses, the Holy Symbols and the Ammunition, are kept as plain lines, one for each row; the Spell Scroll’s two rows are one entry with both prices), the 8 mounts (carrying capacity and price) and the 12 vehicles (the 5 pulled by an animal, with weight and price, and the 7 airborne and waterborne ones, with speed, crew, passengers, cargo, AC, hit points, damage threshold and price), read out as words where the print has only a table row; and left out of the chapter the coins, the Weapon Proficiency, Armor Training and Selling Equipment text, the introductions to the tools, the adventuring gear and the mounts and vehicles (Tool Proficiency, Artisan’s Tools, Mounts and Cargo and the like), the Barding and Saddles text and the rows for saddles (Exotic, Military, Riding), Feed per day and Stabling per day, the notes on large vehicles (Speed, Crew, Passengers, Damage Threshold, Ship Repair), the lifestyle expenses, the food, drink and lodging, the hirelings and the services, the spellcasting services, and the Crafting section, because they are rules about buying and hiring rather than things, which are a later issue;
- split the Gameplay Toolbox chapter (`toolbox.json`) into 87 rules (Travel Pace, Good Roads, Slower Travelers, Extended Travel, Special Movement, Travel Terrain, Vehicles; Curses and Magical Contagions, Bestow Curse, Cursed Creatures, Cursed Magic Items, Narrative Curses, Environmental Curses, Magical Contagions, Rest and Recuperation and the contagions Cackle Fever, Sewer Plague and Sight Rot, each tagged Magical Contagion; the nine Environmental Effects; Fear and Mental Stress, Fear Effects, Mental Stress Effects, Prolonged Effects; Poison, Purchasing Poison, Harvesting Poison; Traps and Parts of a Trap; and the twelve of the Combat Encounters section, each tagged Combat Encounters: Combat Encounters, Combat Encounter Difficulty, Step 1: Choose a Difficulty, Step 2: Determine Your XP Budget, Step 3: Spend Your Budget, Troubleshooting, Many Creatures, Adjustments, CR 0 Creatures, Number of Stat Blocks, Powerful Creatures and Unusual Features; and the 39 of the Magic Items section, each tagged Magic Items, 28 of them the players' own rules (cited in the section Magic Items) and 11 advice to the GM (cited in the Gameplay Toolbox, never sent to a table question: the sentient magic items, and the three box paragraphs Items Made for Specific Creatures, Unusual Anatomy and Paired Items). Where the book's title is an everyday word the rule is named for what it is about: Armor, Potions, Rings, Rods, Scrolls, Staffs, Wands and Weapons are Magic Armor and so on, Charges is Magic Item Charges, “The Next Dawn” is The Next Dawn, Arcana Proficiency, Tools, Spells and Time and Cost are Crafting: Arcana Proficiency, Tools, Spells and Time and Cost, and Abilities, Alignment, Communication, Senses, Special Purpose and Conflict are Sentient Item Abilities and so on. The box of Magic Item Rules is split at its bold lead-ins: Magic Item Rules (its first sentence) and one rule for each paragraph, Attunement Prerequisites, Items Made for Specific Creatures, Unusual Anatomy and Paired Items), 14 poisons (each with its type, Contact, Ingested, Inhaled or Injury, its price for one dose, and its words), 8 sample traps (each with its severity and levels as printed, its trigger, its duration and its words) and the one named curse, Demonic Possession, which the chapter prints inside Environmental Curses (that section keeps the words before it); turned the tables set in the sans fonts into one line for each row, because their cells do not read as sentences when run together: the Magic Item Categories table (“Potions: Potion of Healing”), the rarity values (“Uncommon: 400 GP”, in Magic Item Values by Rarity, with the note on halving the value of a consumable) and the crafting tools (“Ring: Jeweler’s Tools”) and the crafting time and cost (“Rare: 50 days, 2,000 GP”, with its note) of Crafting: Tools and Crafting: Time and Cost, as well as the XP Budget per Character table (“Level 3: Low 150, Moderate 225, High 400 XP per character”, one line for each of the 20 levels, in the text of Step 2: Determine Your XP Budget), the Travel Terrain table (“Forest: Normal Maximum Pace; Encounter Distance 2d8 × 10 feet; Foraging DC 10; Navigation DC 15; Search DC 15”, with its two notes), the Sample Fear DCs and the Sample Mental Stress Effects (“DC 10, 1d6 Psychic damage: …”) in the text of the section they follow, and each trap's table of levels (“Levels 5–10: Bludgeoning Damage 22 (4d10); Save DC 15”); put the four pace formulas, which the PDF boxes with the “=” on a line of its own in the next column, back as lines (“Fast pace = Miles per day × 1⅓ (round down)”) under Special Movement; and left out of the chapter the advice on creating a background (Creating a Background and its five steps, 1: Choose Abilities, 2: Choose a Feat, 3: Choose Skill Proficiencies, 4: Choose a Tool Proficiency and 5: Choose Equipment), the headings Curses (only a pointer to the forms below it), Example Contagions, Sample Poisons, Example Traps and Environmental Effects (each only introduces what is under it), and the tables of random results in the Magic Items section: Potion Miscibility, Sentient Item’s Alignment, Sentient Item’s Communication, Sentient Item’s Senses and Sentient Item’s Special Purpose (the sentence that sends the reader to them is kept, the rows are not); a test pins the list of left-out headings;
- kept the hyphen when a line ends on a number and the next line goes on with a word (“10-” then “foot” is “10-foot”, not “10foot”);
- closed the gap the PDF leaves before a comma after a number (“+7 , reach” is “+7, reach”, “7 ,200” is “7,200”).

Tables and stat blocks inside a spell are kept as plain lines of text: one line for each row of a table (with a space between its columns) and one for each entry of a stat block, however many lines the print wraps it over.

## Making the files again

Download the PDF into a folder of its own (it is not kept in this repository), then, from `core/`:

    pip install -e ".[srd]"
    python -m dmbot.devtools.srd PATH/TO/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf

The same PDF always gives the same files, so running the tool on the PDF and seeing no change (`git diff`) is how to check them. If Wizards of the Coast publishes a new version, the tool stops with a message rather than guessing when the layout is not what it expects.
