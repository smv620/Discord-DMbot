framing: The launchpad: the scene, the location and the party's emotional state at the very start of the session.
framing: The destination: the party's physical position and situation when the session ended, described clearly.
framing: The cliffhanger: if the session ended on a cliffhanger or a moment of tension, the recap ends on it.
travel: Precise travel logs: a short, accurate account of every change of place, with the route, the compass direction and the time the journey took, as the session stated them.
travel: Scene segues: a clean, logical transition whenever the place or the focus changes, so the reader always knows where the new scene is. Say the party left the inn and was on the road before the arrow, not "Then Belleros was struck with an arrow" in a new paragraph.
travel: Valuable reconnaissance: scouting, tracking or investigation that gave the party information of real value.
action: Combat summaries: every combat, summarised, saying how easy or hard the fight was.
action: The near-death gauge: any moment a player character came close to death or faced extreme danger.
action: Loot and asset tracking: every major treasure, magic item or unique asset gained or lost. A large hoard is summed up in a short line.
plot: The element of surprise: unexpected twists, betrayals and shocking dice rolls are always included.
plot: Narrative puzzle pieces: the new clue given, mystery presented or puzzle solved this session. Say only the new step, not the whole history of the mystery.
character: PC-to-PC development: moments between the player characters come first: backstory reveals, flaws shown, new skills learned, and shifts in ideals, bonds or motivations.
character: Weighted NPC interactions: NPC interactions are chosen by how much the NPC matters to the story (background, recurring, significant, major). The ones that matter are included; passing ones are left out.
