The Quiet Choir meets each dusk in the shrine at Caddock Vale, where a low wall of grey stones circles a dry well. Visitors are welcome to stand at the gate and listen. The Choir sings the same three verses every night, and the people of the valley say the songs keep the frost away from the barley.

The Choir is led in public by Mother Ilse Brand, a widow who keeps the shrine's candles and greets each newcomer by name. She has never refused a bowl of soup to a traveller, and the valley's children are taught to bring her wildflowers on feast days.

The real direction of the Choir comes from the groundskeeper, Garrick Fenn, who has tended the shrine for thirty years. Ilse does not know that the verses are a key. Garrick learned them from a book found in the well, and every singer who has joined in the last two winters has been taught a little more of the tune.

Beneath the shrine a stair leads down past the well to a chamber with a bronze door. The Choir believes the door opens when the third verse is sung in full, and Garrick has already tested it once, on a night when the wind was loud enough to hide the sound.

Life in the vale goes on around the shrine. The mill is running again after the summer drought. The road to the market is muddy after rain. The children carry lanterns home at dusk, as they always have.
