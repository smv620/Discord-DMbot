The Temple of Sarn stands on a low island in the Marrowmere, reached by a ferry that crosses the still water twice a day. The temple fell three hundred years ago in the Sundering, when the lake rose over its lower halls. Its bell still hangs in the open arch of the tower, and boatmen ring it to warn of fog.

Travellers come to see the ruins and to leave small offerings on the steps. The ferryman, Halvard Reis, charges a copper for the crossing and a second for the bell. He tells the old story to anyone who asks: the priests of Sarn kept the lake calm with their songs, and when the songs stopped the water came.

The last priest, Ysenne, did not drown in the Sundering. She went down into the sanctum with the temple's relic, a silver cup that holds the lake's calm, and she sealed the stair behind her. Halvard knows this from his grandmother, who saw the cup carried in. Few others believe it.

The stair is still there, hidden behind a fallen column on the north side of the nave. Halvard has cleared the rubble twice and found the door both times. The cup is said to be inside, and whoever carries it out will be able to calm the lake for a season.

Life on the Marrowmere is quiet. The reeds are being cut for the autumn thatch. The ferry's sail was mended in the spring. The fishermen bring in perch and eel on the long days, and the bell is rung only for fog.
