Merrow Hollow is a river city of four thousand souls, ruled by a council of seven seats. Every seat is chosen at the spring fair, when the guilds gather in the square under the old bell tower. The council meets on the first day of each month in the Hall of Reeds, and any citizen may sit in the gallery to listen.

The speaker of the council is Corwen Tadd, a dyer's son who has held the chair for eleven years. He is popular with the mill workers and trusted by the harbour masters, and he keeps the city's accounts in an iron-bound ledger that is read aloud at every session.

Two seats are not what they seem. The seat held by Hesper Lowe is in fact kept by her brother, Dunstan, who wears his sister's face in a veil and signs her name to the minutes. No one in the gallery has been told this. Dunstan has done it since the winter of the flood, when Hesper fell ill and the council feared an empty chair would give the mill guild its vote.

The council also keeps a second, sealed ledger in the cellar under the Hall of Reeds. It lists the debts the city owes to the Varn family, and the names of the three councillors who have been paid to vote with them. Only the speaker and the clerk have the key.

Ordinary matters are simple. The bridge over the Narrow is to be repaired before harvest. The fishing fee rises by one copper in the summer months. The bell is rung for the fair, for a funeral, and for a fire.
