The Guild of Saltwright Carters has carried goods along the Harrow road for two hundred years, under a charter granted by the old king. Its members wear a grey cloak with a white band, and every carter pays a yearly fee at the guildhall on Tanner Street. The guildmaster is chosen each spring by a show of hands.

The current guildmaster is Odelle Pike, a practical woman who is known for fair prices and prompt payment. She has settled every dispute in the last six years without going to court, and the apprentices speak of her with respect. She keeps the guild's books in a plain room above the weigh-house.

Odelle sells information to the thieves' cant that works the north road. Each week a carter who runs the Harrow road drops a sealed note in the poor box at the back of the guildhall, and Odelle passes the names of safe wagons to the cant. The guild's coffers grow by this trade, and the carters are never told where the money comes from.

Beneath the wharf warehouses there is a cellar, reached by a trapdoor under a stack of salt sacks. The cant stores its stolen cloth there, and the guild has used the cellar to hide brine casks that carry a sleeping draught for the watch. Only Odelle and the wharfmaster know the way down.

Most of the guild's work is dull and honest. The road was resurfaced in the spring. The weigh-house scales were checked and certified. The apprentices learn knots on the quay every Thursday.
