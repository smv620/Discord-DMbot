Privacy

The short version

DMbot only records people who said yes.

We never keep the sound of your voice: it is turned into text and the sound is thrown away.

Everyone in your Discord server can read what was written down.

We keep your Discord account number, your name, your email and a list of your servers while you are signed in, so you can sign in and use your plan.

We don't sell anything about you, and there are no ads.

You can stop being recorded at any time, and you can delete your account from My Account.

Who looks after your information

DMbot is run by DMbot LLC. Write to dmbot.admin@gmail.com about anything on this page.

What we keep

When you sign in to this website

  • Your Discord account number, your Discord name and theemail Discord gives us (only if Discord has confirmed it is yours). We use the email for the emails listed under "Email from DMbot" below, and to fill in the payment page when you pick a plan. We keep your Discord name so a personal link we send you can greet you by it.
  • The email our payment company has for your payments, once you have paid. We keep it next to the Discord one so a move code can go to the address you pay with.
  • The names and numbers of the Discord servers you are in, and whether you manage them, so we can show you the ones that use DMbot. We keep this only while you are signed in.
  • You can also sign in with a link we email you. It works once and ends after 15 minutes. We keep only a scrambled copy of it, not the link itself, until then. When you ask for a link, our answer is the same whether or not the email has an account.
  • A personal link from us holds your DMbot ID (your account number) and a secret part that stops working after 30 days. With it, the Prices and Say hello pages greet you by name and show your experience points (the minutes DMbot has run for you). Without the link, those pages show nothing about you. Don't share it.
  • Sign-in uses Discord. We never see your Discord password. Discord gives us a one-time sign-in pass. We use it once to read your account, then cancel it. We never keep it.

If you were given free access

  • Your Discord account number, each time free access was given, changed or taken back, and when, so we can see who had free access. We keep this record after you delete your account.

When DMbot is in your game

  • Your yes. When you press "I consent", we keep the date, the wording you agreed to, and how you agreed. That's how we prove you said yes.
  • What was said. The words of people who said yes, written down as text, with the time they were said. Nobody else is ever recorded, and bots are never recorded.
  • Your campaign. The names, places and notes your table makes, the game master's notes and settings, and any adventure or rulebook text the game master shares. That includes which items are the only one of their kind and who carries what, and whether the game master or the bot said so. A backup holds it, and it is deleted when the campaign is deleted.What DMbot makes from a session belongs to the campaign. That means a recap, story facts, notes for the game master, and anything else DMbot writes from what was said. It is part of the campaign, and the campaign belongs to its game master.
  • What was typed. While DMbot is listening, it also reads what people who said yes type in the table's chat: the voice channel's chat, one more channel the game master picks, and the dmb-chat channel where anyone can ask a quick rules question. Typed lines go in the written record, marked as typed. Questions put to DMbot, and its answers about the game, go in the written record too, marked as such.
  • Names at the table. DMbot notices what people are called at the table, like "Mike", only from people who said yes to being recorded. The game master checks those names. If you press ⚙️ Menu, then Stop recording me, DMbot forgets the names it picked up about you. Names your game master typed in themselves stay.
  • Your voice is not kept. The sound is sent to be written down, then thrown away. It is never saved.

When you use the Say hello page

Your message is posted on GitHub, where anyone can read it. If you tell us how to reach you, we keep that with your message for a year; only the team sees it. To have a post taken down, ask on the same page.

When you write to the team

From your account page, you can write to the DMbot team. That message goes only to the team, privately, and is not posted anywhere public. The team replies by email to the address on your account, if it has one. We keep it for 12 months, then delete it. After you send it, your account page shows you its subject, when you sent it and whether it was answered, never the message itself. If you delete your account, we delete your messages too.

When you pay

Payments go through Stripe. They are the seller, and they hold your card or PayPal details; we never see them. When you pick a plan, we send them your email so their payment page already has it. They tell us your plan, whether it's paid, and when it renews.

Who can see it

  • What was said can be read and downloaded by everyone in the Discord server where the game is played (downloads come with paid plans). The question you say yes to tells you this.
  • The game master's (DM's) notes: each campaign picks who sees them. Only the game master; players who choose to peek (the usual setting, with a spoiler warning first); or everyone in the server. When a player peeks, the game master can see who peeked.
  • Backups of a paid campaign hold the campaign, including the game master's secrets, but not what was said, and anyone in the server may download one. This keeps a campaign safe if its game master leaves.
  • Only people in your server can open your campaign in DMbot. Campaigns never share information, except house rules a game master chooses to share (below).
  • If a game master chooses to share their house rules, only the rules themselves (and the link to their rules document, if they added one) can be read by someone who has the share code. The story, names, notes and what was said are never shared. Tables that use the rules keep their own copy. A table that uses them can suggest a rule back to the game master who shares them. A suggestion carries only the rule's words (and the rule it would replace), never who sent it, and the game master's side keeps no record of the sender.

Email from DMbot

  • Which email. A move code goes only to the email you paid with. A sign-in link goes to the address you type, if it is the one you paid with or the one Discord has for you. The Try It emails go to the email Discord gave us.
  • What we send. A sign-in link and a move code, only when you ask for one, and two Try It emails: one on the last day of Try It, and one if you get 30 more days. A game master also gets an email when a recap is ready or could not be written (see Recaps below). Nothing else.
  • Stopping emails. Each Try It email has a link to stop emails like it. We keep a list of the Discord account numbers that used it, with the date, so we do not send them another. This list stays even after you delete your account, so we never email you again after you said stop. Sign-in links and move codes have no such link, because you asked for them.
  • A short note of what was sent. For each email we keep your Discord account number, which kind of email it was and when it went. We do not keep the words of the email or the address it went to. We use it so a one-time email is never sent twice, and to keep the number of emails we send in a day under a limit. The notes for sign-in links and move codes are deleted after 90 days. The notes for the Try It emails are deleted a year after they were sent. All your notes are deleted when you delete your account.
  • Who sends them. For now the emails go out through DMbot's own Gmail mailbox, so Google carries them. Every Try It email ends with a link to stop emails like it and the sender's mailing address.

Companies that help us

We share only what each one needs to do its job:

  • Discord: where DMbot runs, and how you sign in.
  • Deepgram: turns the sound of people who said yes into text.
  • Anthropic: the AI that helps with names, rules and story notes. It gets the text it needs, not sound. That text isn't used to train Anthropic's AI.
  • Stripe: takes payments.
  • GitHub: where messages from the Say hello page are posted, for anyone to read.
  • Cloudflare: delivers this website, and checks that a person (not a robot) sent a Say hello message; for that it sees your internet address.
  • OVH US LLC (OVHcloud, U.S.A.), a subsidiary of OVH Groupe: the server DMbot and its database run on. Its servers are in the United States.

Deepgram, Anthropic, OVH US, Cloudflare, GitHub and Stripe are companies in the United States, so if you are in the EU or the UK, your information goes there when you use DMbot. Stripe is the seller of record and handles withdrawal rights, invoices and tax.

We don't sell your information and we don't use it for ads.

If a server uses its own AI or speech key, we keep that key locked (encrypted), use it only for that server, and never show it in full.

Recaps

  • What it is. After a session, DMbot can write a short story-style recap of it for the game master. Only paid plans have it.
  • Who writes it. The AI company named above (Anthropic). It gets the text of what people who said yes said in that session (marked only "DM" or "Player"), the names the game master confirmed (never the ones marked secret), the game's last posted recap and the day in the story. It does not get sound. That text isn't used to train Anthropic's AI.
  • Who sees it. Only the game masters of that campaign, until one of them presses Post. Posting puts it in the table's chat channel (dmb-chat), where everyone who can see that channel can read it. DMbot emails a game master when it is ready or could not be written: the email has the title (or the reason) and a link, never the recap, and has no link to stop it. DMbot won't post a recap if a player has stopped being recorded since it was written.
  • How long it is kept. A recap, and the list of what happened that it was made from, are kept with the campaign and deleted when the campaign is deleted. Trying again replaces the old text, and Don't post keeps the draft saved. We keep a short note that the email was sent, with no words in it, and delete it after 90 days. The email itself holds the recap's title and goes through Google, like our other email.
  • Narrated recaps. To make the voice and the music, the words to be read aloud and short descriptions of the music go to ElevenLabs. We do not keep the audio files on our server.

When you delete your account, we delete your account details. We keep only:

  • your Discord account number, if you used the free Try It, so it can't be used twice;
  • the reference numbers of your payments, with your Discord account number, so no payment is counted twice;
  • if you were ever given free access: your Discord account number, each time it was given, changed or taken back, and when, so we can see who had free access.

Stripe, as the seller, keeps the invoices and tax records that payment law requires.

Your choices

  • Stop being recorded at any time: press ⚙️ Menu in DMbot's private message to you, then "Stop recording me", then "Yes". Or type/consent revoke in Discord. DMbot stops recording you and removes what this page says it removes. What DMbot already made from sessions you took part in, such as a recap or the story facts your game master kept, stays with the campaign. Your voice was never kept.
  • Delete your account from My Account. This removes your sign-in details and your plan, straight away. If you pay for a plan, you won't be charged again, and the rest of the month isn't paid back. If you want money back, ask before you delete (see Refunds). Lines you said in someone else's campaign stay there, readable by that server, until that campaign is deleted. What DMbot made from those sessions stays with the campaign too: deleting your account does not remove it. Your campaigns are not deleted with your account, because the other players may still want to read or download them. They are deleted 120 days later, unless another DM of the campaign takes them over. If the owner's Discord account is gone, or the owner has been out of the server for 7 days, DMbot offers the campaign's other game masters a button to take the campaign on. Only a game master who presses it takes over.
  • Delete a campaign. Only the campaign's game master can. Deleting it is the only thing that removes what DMbot made for it, such as its recaps, story facts and notes. A player who wants something removed can ask their game master.
  • Get a copy of your information.Press Download my data in My Account. You get one file with your account, plan and payments, where you said yes to recording, campaign names and character sheets.
  • Ask about your information. You can ask us to show you, correct, send you a copy of, or delete the information we hold about you, by emailing dmbot.admin@gmail.com. We answer within 30 days.

Cookies

This website uses one cookie to keep you signed in, and two short ones (they last 10 minutes) that are used only while you sign in or add DMbot to a server. Other websites can't read them. There are no tracking or ad cookies. The robot check on the Say hello page is run by Cloudflare and may use a cookie of its own to do its job.

Keeping it safe

Everything travels over a locked (encrypted) connection. Keys and secrets are stored encrypted, and our logs hold ids, never what you said or your payment details.

Children

You must be at least 16 to use DMbot.

Changes

If we change what we keep or who sees it, we'll update this page and tell signed-in users. If the question you said yes to changes, DMbot asks you again before it records you.
