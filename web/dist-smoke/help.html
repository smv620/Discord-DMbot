How to use DMbot

Everything happens in Discord. Buttons and commands are written just as DMbot shows them.

Looking for something new? See what's new. Need more help? See getting help.

For the game master

  1. Add DMbot to your Discord server from the Add to Discord page. Add to Discord
  2. Type /dmbot start in any text channel. Then press ▶ Continue last campaign, or ＋ New campaign if this is your first game.
  3. Pick your table's voice channel, then press ▶ Start listening. Each player gets a private message asking if they say yes. Only people who say yes are recorded.
  4. On your DM screen, press ⚙️ Settings to change the game clock, house rules, or who can peek behind the DM screen.
  5. To let DMbot read what people type, press ⚙️ Settings, then Table chat. It reads the voice channel's chat and one more channel you pick, only from people who said yes.
  6. After a game, press 🧑 Check who is who. DMbot notices when someone answers to a name several times ("Mike, what do you do?" then "I run with my sword"). You see who it thinks plays whom and say yes or no. Nothing is used until you say yes.
  7. To say an item, like a magic sword, is the only one, press 🔍 Find a name, open the item, and press ☝️ Only one exists. Press Allow more than one to undo it. An item with its own proper name is taken as the only one until you say otherwise. Only game masters can change this.
  8. To say who has something, open the name, press 🧭 Connect to…, and choose carries. It is saved either way. If it looks odd, for example "Someone else already has that, and there is only one", DMbot says so and will ask you about it after the session. DMbot only points it out. You decide.
  9. Ask a quick question out loud: "Hey DMbot, how long does Bless last?" The short answer is posted in the table's dmb-chat channel and says how sure DMbot is. You have the final say.
  10. When the game is over, type /dmbot stop.
  11. To keep a copy of a campaign, type /dmbot backup (paid campaigns only).
  12. To share your house rules with another table, press ⚙️ Settings, then 📚 Shared rules, then 📤 Share my house rules (paid campaigns only). DMbot gives you a share code. Anyone with the code can read your rules, so give it only to a game master you trust. Press 🔑 New share code to get another one. Old codes keep working; Stop sharing turns them all off. If your table has relationships of its own (kinds of link between people, places and things), DMbot shows them as a list before sharing. Tick the ones to share and press Share with ticked, or press Rules only. Only their names and what they mean go; never your story, your characters' or places' names, or your notes.
  13. To use another table's rules, press ⚙️ Settings, then 📚 Shared rules, then 📥 Use shared rules, and type their code. DMbot shows you the rules first. Press Yes, use this set to keep them. Your own house rules always win over theirs. If the set brings relationships, DMbot tells you which. You can't change them while you use the set. One you already have by the same name stays yours.
  14. When shared rules change, DMbot adds the new version at your next session and tells you. Press See changes to look, Pick changes to choose which to keep, or Undo to put your old copy back. Relationships come one at a time like rules.
  15. To suggest one of your own house rules to the game master whose rules you use, type /dmbot houserules, press 💡 Suggest next to your rule, then press Yes, send it. It goes exactly as written, with no name on it, so check that the rule names no one. Your own rules stay as they are.
  16. You hear the answer on your DM screen at your next session. You only see 💡 Suggest for rules your table wrote, and only while your copy still gets updates.
  17. When another table suggests a rule for the rules you share, a note appears on your DM screen. It never says which table sent it. Press See to preview the change, Add to set to make a new version for every table that uses your rules, or No thanks. Nothing changes until you press Add to set.
  18. If the rules you use stop being shared, your table keeps its copy and the rules keep working, but they won't get updates any more. The 📚 Shared rules screen says "This copy no longer gets updates." To get updates again, ask that game master to share the rules again and send you the new code. Then press Stop using shared rules (your own rules stay, and Undo puts the copy back), then 📥 Use shared rules, and type the code.
  19. A backup keeps the rules your table uses: the set's name, the version you had, and its rules. It does not keep the share code. After /dmbot restore, the Shared rules screen says "This copy no longer gets updates", and the rules keep working. To get updates again, ask the game master for a share code. Press Stop using shared rules, then 📥 Use shared rules, and type the code.
  20. To stop using someone's rules, press ⚙️ Settings, then 📚 Shared rules, then Stop using shared rules. Your own rules stay as they are. Their relationships go too, unless a fact of yours uses one: that one stays, marked, and can't be used for new facts.
  21. To stop sharing your own rules, press ⚙️ Settings, then 📚 Shared rules, then Stop sharing, then Yes, stop sharing. Every share code stops working. Tables already using your rules keep their copy, but it won't be updated any more.
  22. To give a campaign to another game master, press ⚙️ Settings, then Hand over. They must say yes.

What DMbot knows when you ask it

  • Quick answers work while your game is running and you have said yes to being recorded.
  • Message DMbot privately, by voice or by typing, and the answer comes to you alone. Say "Hey DMbot" when the table has a dmb-chat channel, and the answer is posted there for everyone. It then uses only the free rules, your house rules and what was just said. If it needs your story, you get that answer privately.
  • DMbot answers from the free rules, your house rules, what was said in the last few minutes, and the story facts you have confirmed about the names in your question. It leaves out names you marked secret, and your own notes on names and places.
  • If an answer is not from DMbot's rules, it says "check your book". When it has nothing to go on, it says so, for example "I don't have that. Your call." You decide.
  • Your question, and DMbot's answer when it is about the game, are saved in the game's full written record. Everyone in your Discord server can read that record.

Recaps

  • After a session, DMbot can write a short story-style recap of what happened, to read at the start of next time. Recaps are for paid plans.
  • It is written only from what was said by people who said yes to being recorded, and DMbot checks that every detail came from that. It leaves out any name you marked secret. Anything said out loud at the table can still be in it, so read it before you post.
  • DMbot tells you on the DM screen when a recap is ready, or when it couldn't write one and why. It also emails you, unless you turn that off. The email has the title (or the reason) and a link to your account page, never the recap itself.
  • On the DM screen, press Read it to read the recap there. Press Post to the table to send it to your players; DMbot asks once first. Press Not this one to skip it; you can still find it on your recap page. To change the words first, use the link in the message.
  • Press Recaps on ⚙️ Settings to stop the recaps, to stop only the email (the DM screen still tells you), or, on plans with narration, to stop the narration. You can turn them back on there. Your plan decides what you can have.
  • You read it first. You can change the words. Try again writes a new one and throws away your changes; you can do that up to twice. Or press Don't post, which is the same as Not this one on the DM screen. Nothing goes to your players until you press Post to the table.
  • Post to the table puts the recap in your table's chat channel, dmb-chat. It can also list hit points, items and conditions. DMbot checks again first: if a player has stopped being recorded since it was written, or it names something you keep secret, it won't post and tells you why. It can take about a minute.
  • A recap can also come with a narrator's voice and music, as two audio files you play together.
  • Each campaign gets up to 10 recaps a month. Up to 8 of those can be narrated, with a voice and music. If you use them all, DMbot tells you on the DM screen, and the next recap comes on the 1st of next month.
  • Narration comes with Guild and Pro. On the other paid plans it is an add-on. If the audio comes out wrong, ask for it again on the recap page. You can do that once every 10 minutes.

For players

  1. DMbot sends you a private message. Press I'm 16 or older, record me to say yes.
  2. Want to say no? Press No thanks. You can still play.
  3. To stop at any time, press ⚙️ Menu in that private message, then Stop recording me, then Yes, stop recording me.
  4. While DMbot is listening, it also reads what you type in the table's chat, if you said yes. Nothing is read from anyone who said no.
  5. Anyone at the table who said yes can ask DMbot a quick rules question: say "Hey DMbot" or type it in the dmb-chat channel. Your question is posted there with your name. Your game master has the final say.
  6. When you ask DMbot a question in dmb-chat, it shows the name of the character you play once your game master has said yes to it. Until then it shows your server nickname.
  7. Everyone in the server can read a game's transcript. To read or download one, type /transcript.
  8. Press 📜 My character sheet to link your online sheet, or to type your character in.
  9. A linked sheet must be set to Public on the sheet's own site, so anyone with the link can open it. Your game master sees it on your character's card.
  10. In a fight, DMbot's fight helper, FightBot, may send you a short private reminder of what your sheet says you still have, like a spell, a feature, or spell slots. It only reminds you. Your game master decides what happens. To switch it off, press 📜 My character sheet, then Turn FightBot off.
  11. If your sheet is linked online, your spell slots show on your character card. About a minute after someone at the table says a slot was used, DMbot looks at your sheet again to keep up. It never changes your sheet.

Your game master may show the story's day and time, like "Day 5, morning", in your own channel. You do not need to do anything.

Your account

  1. Forgot your password? DMbot has no password. It uses your Discord account, so use Discord's own reset: open the Discord sign-in page and press Forgot your password?
  2. Moving to a new Discord account? Do this for each campaign you own: press ⚙️ Settings, then press Hand over, and pick your new account or another game master. When all are handed over, cancel your plan on your account page. On the new account, say yes to being recorded again.
  3. Can't get into your old account any more? After 7 days out of the server, or if Discord says the account is gone, DMbot offers the campaign's other game masters a Take it on button in ⚙️ Settings. A game master chooses whether to press it. Or use a new account: join the server, type /dmbot backup, then /dmbot restore. Both need a paid campaign, and restore needs a free campaign slot on your plan.

Emails from DMbot

  1. DMbot sends very few emails. A sign-in link and a move code come only when you ask for one. The only others are two Try It emails, each sent once, and an email to a game master when a recap is ready or could not be written.
  2. Sign-in link: ask for it on your account page and type the email you paid with or the email Discord has for you. We send a link that works once and stops working after 15 minutes.
  3. Move code: when you move to a new Discord account, run /dmbot migrate on your old account and press Get a move code. It goes to the email you paid with, and nowhere else. It works once and lasts 30 minutes. A move code is only for you: never give it to anyone.
  4. Didn't ask for a sign-in link or a move code? Ignore the email. Nothing changes.
  5. Try It emails: one on the last day of Try It, and one if you get 30 more days. Each has a link to stop emails like it. Sign-in and move-code emails have no such link, because you asked for them.
  6. Recap emails: DMbot emails you when a recap is ready, or when it couldn't write one. Each has the title (or the reason) and a link. Only the game master gets them, once for each recap. They have no link to stop them.

For now, emails are sent from DMbot's own Gmail mailbox.

Getting help

  1. Look here first. The steps above may answer it.
  2. Ask in public. Post a question in the Q&A for DMbot. A helper called QAbot (a bot, not a person) may answer first. Everything there is public, so don't post account or payment details.
  3. Have an account? Write to the team in private from your account page. The team replies by email to the address on your account, if it has one.
  4. Anyone, on any plan. Email support@getdmbot.com.

Stuck? Say hello and tell us what you saw.
