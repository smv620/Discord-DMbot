What DMbot does at your table

Quiet tips for the DM

DMbot listens to the people who said yes and sends the game master a private tip with its source. It never makes up the story. The game master decides.

Someone casts a spell. The DM sees the rule and where it comes from, on the DM screen.

Only people who said yes

Nobody is recorded until they say yes. That covers what you say and what you type in the table's chat. Anyone can stop at any time.

To stop, press ⚙️ Menu in DMbot's private message, then Stop recording me.

Transcripts anyone can read

Everyone in your Discord server can read and download the written record of each game.

Missed last week? Read what was said before the game starts.

Ask "Hey DMbot"

Anyone at the table who said yes can ask a quick rules question out loud or in the dmb-chat channel. The short answer is posted there, and the game master has the final say. Questions and answers go in the written record.

"Hey DMbot, how long does Bless last?" The answer says how sure DMbot is.

The game clock

Players see the story's day and time in their own channel. The game master sees the exact time.

Players see "Day 5, morning". The game master can time a spell like Bless.

Remembers your story

Names, places and characters your game master confirms are kept from one game to the next, along with which items are one of a kind and who carries them. DMbot only points out when something doesn't add up, and never makes up story.

The innkeeper from last month is still the innkeeper. You said there is only one magic sword, so DMbot flags it if two people carry it.

Character sheets

Players link their public online sheet to their character, and the game masters see it on the character's card.

No more asking "what's on your sheet?" in the middle of a fight.

Backups (paid plans)

A campaign is never lost if its game master leaves. Anyone in the server can download a backup, which holds the game master's secrets.

A new game master loads the backup and carries on.

Share house rules (paid plans)

A game master on a paid plan can choose to share their house rules with another table using a share code. The other table can use them, keep its own changes, and suggest a rule back. Only the rules are shared, never the story, names or what was said.

A friend's table starts with your house rules, then tweaks the ones that don't fit their game.

Before you start

Does it record everyone?

No. Only people who press yes (16 or older) are recorded, for what they say and type, and bots never are. Anyone can stop at any time with ⚙️ Menu, then Stop recording me; what was already written stays in the record.

Can my players see my DM notes?

You choose. Only you; players can peek after a spoiler warning (the usual setting, and you can see who peeked); or everyone in the server. Server owners and admins can always see every channel, and a paid campaign's backup holds your notes too.

Will DMbot take over my game?

No. It sends you quiet tips with where they come from, and you decide. It never makes up story.

Who can read the game's written record?

Everyone in your Discord server can read and download it. The message that asks players to say yes tells them so, and says it stays there even if they stop later. Read the privacy page

Does it send what we say to an AI?

Yes, in two steps: a speech company (Deepgram) turns the sound of people who said yes into text, and the sound is thrown away. An AI company (Anthropic) then gets the text it needs, not sound, to help with names, rules and story notes. That text isn't used to train Anthropic's AI. Read the privacy page

Which rules does it use?

The newest rules first, and older ones are marked Legacy. Your own house rules come first.

What do I need?

A Discord server where you can add DMbot, and a voice channel for your table. You choose the channel it listens in. Plans are on the Prices page. See the plans
